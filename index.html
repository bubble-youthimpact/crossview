Climate

Climate Responsibility — A Structured Dialogue

🌏Developing Countries
CORE PRINCIPLES Historical & Structural

Responsibility should reflect equity — vulnerability and historical emissions must matter when defining fairness.

DEVELOPMENT STAGE Leadership from First Beneficiaries

Industrialized nations grew using fossil fuels. Developing nations are still lifting millions out of poverty; leadership must come from those who benefited first.

CONSUMPTION VS TERRITORIAL Consumption Counts

A significant share of emissions comes from producing goods consumed in developed countries. Consumption should be part of responsibility.

🏭Developed Countries
CORE PRINCIPLES Current & Measurable

Future warming depends on current major emitters; without strong action from all large economies, global targets will not be met.

DEVELOPMENT STAGE Evolving Responsibility

Global emissions today are driven significantly by emerging economies, so responsibility must evolve with changing realities.

CONSUMPTION VS TERRITORIAL Territorial Accounting

International climate agreements measure territorial emissions; expanding to consumption complicates enforcement and accountability.

Key framing difference

Developing countries frame responsibility as historical, structural, and capacity-based. Developed countries frame it as current, measurable, and shared among major emitters.

APEX · June 2026

Managed Rivalry: China, India, and the Limits of the 2025 Thaw

For most of the period following the 2020 Galwan Valley clash, China-India relations were defined by what they lacked: high-level visits, direct flights, completed disengagement at key friction points, and even a shared understanding of the border dispute. That changed in 2025. A series of disengagement agreements, ministerial exchanges, and an August 2025 summit between Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Tianjin, the first visit by an Indian leader to China in seven years, produced the most substantial normalization of bilateral relations since 2020.

The Price of Cheap Clothing: Labor Vulnerability Across Asia’s Garment Supply Chains

Asia’s garment supply chains generate substantial value for global fashion brands, yet much of the social and economic cost falls on workers with the least power to resist. This brief examines labor vulnerability in four major garment-producing economies: Bangladesh, Cambodia, Myanmar, and Vietnam. Brands set prices and delivery schedules, suppliers absorb the resulting pressure, and workers bear it through low wages, unpaid or excessive overtime, insecure contracts, and restrictions on organizing.

Read briefAPEX · May 2025

Revolution Without Resolution: Myanmar's Civil War, the Humanitarian Crisis, and the Failures of the International Response

Five years after the February 2021 military coup, Myanmar remains trapped in a worsening catastrophe. What began as a political rupture, involving the arrest of elected leader Aung San Suu Kyi and the seizure of power by Senior General Min Aung Hlaing's Tatmadaw, has evolved into one of the most severe humanitarian crises in modern Southeast Asian history. In 2025 alone, more than 15,000 people were killed in conflict-related violence, nearly 5.2 million people were displaced, and 22 million required humanitarian assistance. A magnitude 7.7 earthquake in March 2025 further compounded an already catastrophic situation.
