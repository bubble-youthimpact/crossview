Geopolitics

China's Growing Influence in the Asian Region

🇵🇭Philippines
SECURITY PERCEPTION Direct Security Threat

China is viewed as an immediate danger due to repeated maritime confrontations in the West Philippine Sea, making security a matter of urgency and survival.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Sovereignty at Stake

The issue is framed around territorial integrity and national dignity, with the West Philippine Sea treated as a non-negotiable red line.

ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS Security Over Economy

Economic ties with China are secondary when national sovereignty and maritime rights are perceived to be under threat.

POLICY & DIPLOMATIC APPROACH Public & Legal Pushback

The Philippines relies on diplomatic protests, international law, and alliances to challenge China's actions.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Urgent & Confrontational

Media coverage emphasizes incidents, violations, and national rights, reinforcing a sense of immediate threat.

🇰🇷South Korea
SECURITY PERCEPTION Potential Security Threat

China is seen as a possible security concern, but one that should be handled cautiously to avoid regional instability and diplomatic issues.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Strategic Balance Management

China is framed as a powerful actor that must be carefully managed through balance and risk containment rather than escalation.

ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS Economic Interdependence

As China is South Korea's largest trading partner, economic stability strongly influences Seoul's cautious approach.

POLICY & DIPLOMATIC APPROACH Diplomatic Restraint

South Korea prioritizes quiet diplomacy and strategic ambiguity to maintain stability and economic relations.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Cautious and Analytical

Media focuses on long-term strategy and regional balance, avoiding highly confrontational language.

Climate

Climate Responsibility — A Structured Dialogue

🌏Developing Countries
CORE PRINCIPLES Historical & Structural

Responsibility should reflect equity — vulnerability and historical emissions must matter when defining fairness.

DEVELOPMENT STAGE Leadership from First Beneficiaries

Industrialized nations grew using fossil fuels. Developing nations are still lifting millions out of poverty; leadership must come from those who benefited first.

CONSUMPTION VS TERRITORIAL Consumption Counts

A significant share of emissions comes from producing goods consumed in developed countries. Consumption should be part of responsibility.

CLIMATE FINANCE Finance Is Non-Negotiable

Without predictable finance, a just transition is politically and economically unrealistic. Responsibility must include enabling capacity.

🏭Developed Countries
CORE PRINCIPLES Current & Measurable

Future warming depends on current major emitters; without strong action from all large economies, global targets will not be met.

DEVELOPMENT STAGE Evolving Responsibility

Global emissions today are driven significantly by emerging economies, so responsibility must evolve with changing realities.

CONSUMPTION VS TERRITORIAL Territorial Accounting

International climate agreements measure territorial emissions; expanding to consumption complicates enforcement and accountability.

CLIMATE FINANCE Shared Ambition

Finance matters, but ambition and reductions must be shared across all major emitters, not just historic ones.

Key framing difference

Developing countries frame responsibility as historical, structural, and capacity-based. Developed countries frame it as current, measurable, and shared among major emitters.

Environment

Commercial Whaling

🇺🇸🇪🇺US & EU
CORE PERCEPTION Environmental Threat

Whaling is viewed as a direct threat to marine ecosystems and biodiversity.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Conservation & Ethics

Whaling is framed as unethical and harmful to endangered species, requiring strict global protection.

ECONOMICS & SCIENCE Protection Over Use

Economic or cultural benefits do not justify ecological damage.

POLICY & GOVERNANCE International Regulation

Supports global bans and institutions like the IWC to enforce restrictions.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Critical & Normative

Focus on ethics, conservation, and global responsibility.

🇯🇵Japan
CORE PERCEPTION Cultural Practice

Whaling is seen as a long-standing cultural tradition and regulated activity.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Sovereignty & Tradition

Whaling is framed as a sovereign right tied to cultural identity and food practices.

ECONOMICS & SCIENCE Regulated Sustainability

Limited whaling is justified through scientific management and population control.

POLICY & GOVERNANCE National Control

Rejects external control; prioritizes domestic regulation over international pressure.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Defensive & Justifying

Focus on cultural legitimacy, sovereignty, and scientific claims.

Culture

Cultural Consumption Asymmetry

🇰🇷South Korea
CORE PERCEPTION Low Consumption & Visibility

Filipino culture has minimal presence in South Korean media and markets.

AUDIENCE PREFERENCE No Comparable Data

No evidence of strong Korean preference for Filipino media or entertainment.

MUSIC & CONCERT MARKET Minimal Market Presence

No Filipino artists in Korean top charts. No confirmed solo concerts in Korea. Limited to festival appearances only.

MEDIA & PLATFORM VISIBILITY Near Absence

Filipino music and artists largely absent from Korean streaming charts. Philippines is often portrayed with stereotypes such as crime or corruption settings rather than as a cultural subject.

PUBLIC PERCEPTION Neutral Awareness

Perception of the Philippines is mostly neutral (~57% neutral responses), with limited evidence of strong cultural interest.

🇵🇭Philippines
CORE PERCEPTION High Consumption & Preference

Filipino audiences actively consume and prefer Korean popular culture.

AUDIENCE PREFERENCE Strong, Measurable Demand

100% preferred Korean drama storylines. 95% rated actors/characters more appealing. 88.9% positive view of Korean pop culture. 91.4% positive view of South Korea.

MUSIC & CONCERT MARKET Active Market Engagement

Regular K-pop concerts in the Philippines. World tours consistently include Manila. OST-focused concerts (e.g., K-drama music events).

MEDIA & PLATFORM VISIBILITY High Cultural Penetration

Korean music, dramas, and artists widely consumed across platforms. Korean content is consumed as mainstream entertainment.

PUBLIC PERCEPTION Strong Positive Affinity

88.9% favorability toward Korean culture and 91.4% toward the country, reflecting strong cultural affinity and engagement.

Key framing difference

Cultural exchange is not reciprocal — influence flows primarily in one direction. Korean culture is actively consumed in the Philippines, while Filipino culture remains marginal in South Korea.

Environment

Fukushima Treated Water Release

🇯🇵Japan
SECURITY PERCEPTION Scientifically Safe

Treated water meets international standards and is below radiation limits.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Technical Necessity

Release is necessary for plant decommissioning and storage limitations.

ENVIRONMENTAL STANDARDS Global Compliance

Highlights IAEA approval as international validation.

PUBLIC & POLITICAL REACTION Data-Based Communication

Emphasizes monitoring systems and transparency.

LONG-TERM IMPACT Controlled Monitoring

Stresses continuous radiation measurement over decades.

🇰🇷South Korea
SECURITY PERCEPTION Safety with Caution

Accepts scientific review but stresses uncertainty and monitoring.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Public Confidence Issue

Focus on social trust, domestic reassurance, and risk communication.

ENVIRONMENTAL STANDARDS Reference, Not Final Word

Respects IAEA review but maintains independent verification.

PUBLIC & POLITICAL REACTION Public Debate & Anxiety

Media highlights seafood safety and political controversy.

LONG-TERM IMPACT Precautionary Monitoring

Focus on possible cumulative marine impact.

🌱Greenpeace
SECURITY PERCEPTION Not Proven Safe

Argues long-term ecological effects remain uncertain and insufficiently studied.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Environmental Justice

Frames discharge as avoidable and prioritizes marine ecosystem protection.

ENVIRONMENTAL STANDARDS Standards Are Not Enough

Claims regulatory compliance does not equal ecological safety.

PUBLIC & POLITICAL REACTION Activism & Campaigning

Organizes protests and international advocacy efforts.

LONG-TERM IMPACT Irreversible Ocean Risk

Warns about bioaccumulation and long-term ecosystem damage.

Technology

Huawei — Security vs Suppression

🇺🇸United States
SECURITY CONCERNS Critical Infrastructure Risk

U.S. officials argued Huawei's involvement in 5G networks could expose sensitive data to the Chinese government.

TECHNOLOGY COMPETITION Protecting Tech Leadership

Restrictions were framed as necessary to safeguard American technological dominance.

GOVERNMENT ROLE State Responsibility to Act

The U.S. government presented the blacklist as a precautionary defense measure.

GLOBAL IMPLICATIONS Preventing Systemic Risk

The issue was portrayed as part of broader global security concerns.

🇨🇳China
SECURITY CONCERNS No Proven Threat

China rejected the accusations, stating that no evidence had been provided to support security claims.

TECHNOLOGY COMPETITION Blocking China's Rise

China described the measures as an attempt to slow its technological development.

GOVERNMENT ROLE Political Suppression

Chinese officials argued the U.S. was using state power to target a private company.

GLOBAL IMPLICATIONS Undermining Free Trade

China framed the restrictions as harmful to global innovation and open markets.

Technology

Huawei Issue — Expanded (US, China, Canada)

🇺🇸United States
SECURITY PERCEPTION Direct Security Threat

Huawei is viewed as a risk to 5G networks and critical infrastructure due to potential state-linked data access.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Infrastructure Protection

The issue is framed around protecting critical national infrastructure and data sovereignty.

ECONOMIC DIMENSION Strategic Tech Competition

Restrictions are justified as protecting domestic technological leadership.

GOVERNMENT ROLE Protective Intervention

Government action is framed as necessary to defend national interests.

DIPLOMATIC IMPACT Alliance Coordination

Encouraged allies to restrict Huawei in 5G networks.

🇨🇳China
SECURITY PERCEPTION No Proven Evidence

China rejects espionage claims and argues that security accusations lack concrete proof.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Political Suppression of Tech

The dispute is framed as an attempt to suppress China's technological growth.

ECONOMIC DIMENSION Disruption of Fair Trade

U.S. sanctions are described as harmful to global trade and market competition.

GOVERNMENT ROLE Abuse of State Power

The U.S. government is portrayed as using state authority to target a private company.

DIPLOMATIC IMPACT Defense of Sovereignty

Accused the U.S. of politicizing technology and undermining cooperation.

🇨🇦Canada
SECURITY PERCEPTION Potential Vulnerability

Canada treats Huawei as a possible security risk, aligning concerns with intelligence partners.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Regulatory & Legal Compliance

The issue is presented as a matter of national security standards and legal responsibility.

ECONOMIC DIMENSION Security Over Market Access

Economic impact acknowledged, but security concerns take priority.

GOVERNMENT ROLE Rule of Law Process

Decisions are framed as legal obligations, not political targeting.

DIPLOMATIC IMPACT Tension Management

Balanced alliance commitments while managing strained relations with China.

Security

North Korea's Nuclear Program

🇰🇷🇯🇵South Korea & Japan
SECURITY PERCEPTION Direct Security Threat

North Korea's nuclear weapons and missile tests are viewed as a direct threat to regional and global security, endangering allies and the nonproliferation system.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Violation of International Rules

Framed as a violation of international law and UN resolutions. Allowing North Korea nuclear weapons undermines the global nonproliferation regime.

STRATEGIC APPROACH Deterrence and Sanctions

Promotes economic sanctions, strong military deterrence, and closer alliance cooperation to pressure denuclearization.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Security-Focused Reporting

Coverage highlights missile launches, military threats, and the importance of deterrence and international rules.

🇨🇳🇷🇺China & Russia
SECURITY PERCEPTION Stability Concern

Acknowledge the nuclear issue but frame it primarily as a problem of regional instability; excessive pressure risks escalation.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Result of Regional Tensions

Argue the nuclear program is partly a reaction to security pressures, especially U.S. military presence, and must be understood in geopolitical context.

STRATEGIC APPROACH Dialogue and Negotiation

Emphasize diplomatic negotiations, security guarantees, and gradual dialogue as the most effective path.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Stability-Oriented Reporting

Coverage stresses avoiding escalation, criticizing sanctions, and promoting diplomatic dialogue.

CORE PERCEPTION Corruption Risk

Project viewed as potentially vulnerable to fraud and misuse of public funds.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Transparency & Accountability

Framed as a precautionary action to protect taxpayer money and ensure governance integrity.

ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS Fiscal Responsibility First

Preventing financial risk is prioritized over project continuation.

POLICY & DIPLOMATIC APPROACH Preemptive Suspension

Immediate halt to avoid risk and reinforce oversight standards.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Investigative & Critical

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Sovereignty & Clarification

Framed as a defense of national credibility and correction of misinformation.

ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS Alternative Development Paths

Focus on securing funding from other partners and maintaining project autonomy.

POLICY & DIPLOMATIC APPROACH Diplomatic Response

Requests clarification, denies allegations, and manages bilateral tensions.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Defensive & Reframing

Media emphasizes denial, transparency, and challenges external claims.

Migration

Overseas Labor Migration

🇰🇷South Korea
CORE PERCEPTION Labor System Necessity

Migrant labor is seen as essential to sustain the economy and workforce.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Economic Survival + Rights Overlay

Migration is framed as necessary for labor shortages, while increasingly incorporating human rights concerns.

GOVERNANCE & SYSTEM DESIGN Quota-Based Labor Management

Expansion of visa quotas (e.g., seasonal workers) to address demographic decline and labor shortages.

POLICY & INSTITUTIONAL APPROACH Expansion + Regulation Balance

Simultaneously expands migrant intake while introducing safeguards against exploitation.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Pragmatic + Ethical

Economic urgency dominates, but coverage increasingly highlights discrimination concerns, human dignity, and the need for labor protections.

🇵🇭Philippines
CORE PERCEPTION Citizen Protection Issue

Overseas workers are seen as vulnerable citizens requiring state protection.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING Rights & State Responsibility

Migration is framed around protecting workers from exploitation, illegal recruitment, and abuse.

GOVERNANCE & SYSTEM DESIGN Centralized Protection Mechanisms

Stronger government control over recruitment to eliminate illegal brokers and close loopholes.

POLICY & INSTITUTIONAL APPROACH Regulation & Enforcement

Focus on anti-illegal recruitment laws, monitoring agencies, and accountability systems.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Protective & Rights-Focused

Coverage emphasizes abuse cases, worker vulnerability, and state duty.

Migration

Refugee Acceptance in Europe

🇩🇪Germany & Nordic
MIGRATION PERCEPTION Humanitarian Protection

Refugees are viewed primarily as people in need of international protection.

ISSUE FRAMING Human Rights Responsibility

Accepting refugees is presented as part of Europe's moral and legal obligations.

SOCIAL & ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS Integration Investment

Policies focus on education, employment access, and long-term integration.

POLICY APPROACH Asylum & EU Cooperation

Support for shared EU responsibility and refugee relocation programs.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Humanitarian Narrative

Coverage emphasizes refugee protection and international solidarity.

🇵🇱Eastern Europe
MIGRATION PERCEPTION Security Concern
ISSUE FRAMING Sovereignty & Control

Migration policy is framed as a matter of national sovereignty and border control.

SOCIAL & ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS Capacity Limits

Governments emphasize social capacity and economic burden of large inflows.

POLICY APPROACH Border Enforcement

Stronger border control and resistance to EU-wide refugee quotas.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Security Narrative

Media often highlights border protection and domestic political concerns.

THAAD is viewed as a defensive system against North Korea's missile threat.

CORE ISSUE FRAMING National Self-Defense

STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS Deterrence Stability

Technology

TikTok

🇺🇸United States
DATA SECURITY Protecting User Data

Lawmakers argued that TikTok's ownership structure posed risks to American user data.

POLITICAL FRAMING Guarding Against Foreign Influence

The debate was linked to concerns about algorithmic manipulation and public opinion.

POLICY RESPONSE Forced Sale or Ban

Proposals focused on divestment or restricting the platform's U.S. operations.

BROADER IMPACT National Security First

Security considerations outweighed commercial interests.

DATA SECURITY Commercial Platform, Not a Threat

China maintained that TikTok operates as a private company without government interference.

POLITICAL FRAMING Politicizing Business

China described the restrictions as politically motivated and protectionist.

POLICY RESPONSE Opposition to Coercion

BROADER IMPACT Damage to Global Business Climate

Economy

Trade & Export Asymmetry

🇰🇷South Korea
TRADE FLOW High Export Flow

Korean goods, including beauty products, are exported at large scale, showing strong and consistent market demand.

CULTURAL GOODS Stable High Demand

Korean beauty exports to the Philippines reached ≈ $28.1M (2022) → $29.9M (2023) → $30.0M (2024), showing consistent growth.

TRADE BALANCE Export-Dominant Position

Korea maintains a strong export surplus in the bilateral relationship.

TRADE RATIO Higher Outbound Flow

Korean exports to the Philippines significantly exceed imports.

STRUCTURAL PATTERN Export Strength

South Korea functions as a dominant exporter, with strong market penetration across consumer goods.

🇵🇭Philippines
TRADE FLOW Lower Export Flow

Philippine exports to Korea remain significantly smaller, indicating a weaker trade presence.

CULTURAL GOODS No Comparable Category

There is no equivalent Filipino cultural goods export segment reaching Korea at scale.

TRADE BALANCE Import-Dominant Position

The Philippines imports about $10.02B from Korea, reflecting strong reliance on Korean goods.

TRADE RATIO Higher Inbound Flow

Philippine imports from Korea are approximately 2.26× higher than Korea's imports from the Philippines.

STRUCTURAL PATTERN Import Reliance

The Philippines acts primarily as a consumer market, with less influence in bilateral trade flows.

Economy

Trump-Era Tariffs

🇺🇸United States
POLICY JUSTIFICATION Protecting National Industries

The administration argued that steel and aluminum imports threatened U.S. national security and weakened domestic production capacity.

ECONOMIC PRIORITIES Reducing Trade Imbalance

Tariffs were presented as a tool to shrink trade deficits and bring manufacturing jobs back to the United States.

ALLIANCE CONSIDERATIONS Economics Over Alliance

Economic protection was prioritized even if it created temporary tension with allies.

TRADE PHILOSOPHY Strategic Leverage

Trade was treated as a bargaining tool to gain concessions and improve the U.S. economic position.

🇰🇷South Korea
POLICY JUSTIFICATION Unfair Use of Security Claims

South Korea viewed the tariffs as inappropriate toward a close security ally and questioned the national security rationale.

ECONOMIC PRIORITIES Maintaining Export Stability

As an export-driven economy, South Korea focused on minimizing disruption and protecting long-term trade stability.

ALLIANCE CONSIDERATIONS Alliance Trust at Risk

Korean officials emphasized that such measures could undermine trust between long-standing partners.

TRADE PHILOSOPHY Rule-Based Order

South Korea stressed the importance of WTO norms and predictable multilateral trade rules.

Geopolitics

Ukraine War & Russian Energy

🇺🇸🇪🇺United States & EU
ENERGY SECURITY FRAMING Moral Responsibility

Russian energy exports are framed as financing the war in Ukraine. Reducing imports is presented as part of defending international order.

ISSUE FRAMING War Financing Concern

Buying Russian energy is often linked to indirectly funding military aggression.

ECONOMIC PRIORITIES Energy Diversification

Countries seek alternative suppliers to reduce dependence on Russian energy.

POLICY APPROACH Sanctions & Price Caps

Energy sanctions and oil price caps are used to limit Russia's war revenue.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Normative & Political

Media often links energy policy with moral responsibility and international law.

🌍Global South
ENERGY SECURITY FRAMING Economic Necessity

Russian oil and gas are treated as affordable and reliable energy sources. Energy trade is viewed primarily through domestic economic needs.

ISSUE FRAMING Development Priority

Energy imports are separated from geopolitical conflict and treated as economic policy.

ECONOMIC PRIORITIES Price Stability

Affordable energy supply is prioritized to protect economic growth and consumers.

POLICY APPROACH Continued Energy Trade

Governments maintain imports while avoiding direct involvement in sanctions.

MEDIA & POLITICAL TONE Pragmatic & Economic

Coverage focuses on fuel prices, inflation, and economic stability.
